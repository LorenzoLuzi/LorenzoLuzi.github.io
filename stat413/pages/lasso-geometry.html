Why Lasso Zeroes Coefficients: the Diamond vs the Disk

Real data again: \(\text{mpg}\) (centered, so there is no intercept to learn) regressed on the two best predictors, standardized \(\text{wt}\) and \(\text{qsec}\). The gray ellipses are the contours of \(\text{MSE}(\beta_1, \beta_2)\) and the green star is the OLS optimum. Both penalties can be written as a budget constraint: the solution (black dot) is the point inside the shaded region where the lowest MSE contour (purple) first touches it. Shrink the budget \(t\): the diamond gets touched at a corner, pinning \(\beta_2 = 0\) exactly — the disk never does that.

Budget
on this data the diamond solution sits on a corner for all \(t \lesssim 3.2\); the constraint goes slack past \(\|\hat{\beta}_{OLS}\|\) (L1: 6.50, L2: 5.13)
\( \text{Lasso (constrained):} \quad \hat{\beta}(t) = \arg\min_{\beta}\; \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big(y_i - \beta_1 z_{i1} - \beta_2 z_{i2}\Big)^2 \quad \text{s.t.} \quad |\beta_1| + |\beta_2| \le t \)
\( \text{Ridge (constrained):} \quad \hat{\beta}(t) = \arg\min_{\beta}\; \text{MSE}(\beta) \quad \text{s.t.} \quad \beta_1^2 + \beta_2^2 \le t^2 \)
These are equivalent to the penalized forms \( \text{MSE}(\beta) + \lambda \|\beta\|_1 \) and \( \text{MSE}(\beta) + \lambda \|\beta\|_2^2 \) from the Regularization Paths applet: every budget \(t\) corresponds to some \(\lambda\) (small \(t\) ↔ large \(\lambda\)). The corner is why lasso induces sparsity and ridge does not.
