Gaussian Mixture Models: a Sandbox

A single Gaussian is fully determined by its mean and sd — so it can never be bimodal, boxy, or skewed, even when those shapes have exactly the same mean and sd. A mixture of Gaussians can.

\( p(x) = \sum_{m=1}^{M} \pi_m\, N(x;\, \mu_m, \sigma_m^2), \qquad \pi_m \ge 0,\ \sum_m \pi_m = 1. \qquad \gamma_m(x) = \dfrac{\pi_m N(x;\, \mu_m, \sigma_m^2)}{\sum_{j=1}^{M} \pi_j N(x;\, \mu_j, \sigma_j^2)} \)
\(\gamma_m(x)\) is the responsibility of component \(m\) at \(x\): its weighted curve height there as a fraction of the total mixture height \(p(x)\); the \(\gamma_m(x)\) sum to 1 over \(m\).
