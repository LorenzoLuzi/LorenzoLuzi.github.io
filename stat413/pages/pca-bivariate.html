PCA on a Bivariate Normal

For a centered bivariate normal, PCA needs no algorithm at all: we have a closed form solution.

\( \Sigma = \begin{bmatrix} \sigma_x^2 & r\sigma_x\sigma_y \\ r\sigma_x\sigma_y & \sigma_y^2 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad p(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{1}{2\pi \sigma_x \sigma_y \sqrt{1-r^2}} \exp\!\left( -\frac{1}{2(1-r^2)} \left[ \frac{x^2}{\sigma_x^2} - \frac{2rxy}{\sigma_x\sigma_y} + \frac{y^2}{\sigma_y^2} \right] \right) \)
\( \lambda_\pm = \dfrac{\sigma_x^2 + \sigma_y^2 \pm \sqrt{(\sigma_x^2 - \sigma_y^2)^2 + 4r^2\sigma_x^2\sigma_y^2}}{2}, \qquad K_\pm = \dfrac{\sigma_y^2 - \sigma_x^2 \pm \sqrt{(\sigma_x^2 - \sigma_y^2)^2 + 4r^2\sigma_x^2\sigma_y^2}}{2r\sigma_x\sigma_y} \)
\( \phi_{11} = \sqrt{\tfrac{1}{1+K_+^2}}, \quad \phi_{21} = \mathrm{sign}(K_+)\sqrt{1-\phi_{11}^2}; \qquad \phi_{12} = \sqrt{\tfrac{1}{1+K_-^2}}, \quad \phi_{22} = \mathrm{sign}(K_-)\sqrt{1-\phi_{12}^2} \)  —  and multiplying either column by \(-1\) is still a solution (faint arrows).
